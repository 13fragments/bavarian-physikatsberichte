## Single post

Lower Franconia, around 1860. In his list of local medicinal plants, the district doctor of Klingenberg on the Main notes an export: blackberry leaves "go in great quantity to North America, probably for the adulteration of green tea."

## Short thread

1. Around 1860, every Bavarian district physician had to describe his district, down to its medicinal plants. In Klingenberg on the Main, the list includes a trade note.

2. "The juniper berry is traded; so are the leaves of the blackberry, which go in great quantity to North America, probably for the adulteration of green tea."

3. He does not say who bought the leaves or how much. "Probably" is his own word. But the guess fits the time: cheap leaves dried and sold as tea were a well-known fraud in the nineteenth century.

4. Source: the 1858-1861 Bavarian district physicians' reports (Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, Cgm 6874), open data: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24523214

## Original German

- Klingenberg (`bsb00145112` canvas 101): "Die Wachholderbeere kömmt in den Handel; ebenso die Blätter der Brombeere, die in großer Quantität nach Nordamerika, wahrscheinlich zur Fälschung des grünen Thees, gehen [...]"

## Caveats

- The tea fraud is the physician's guess ("wahrscheinlich"). He gives no quantity, buyer, or source.
- The plant is the blackberry (*Brombeere*). The same entry names raspberry and bilberry separately.
- Thread part 3 makes a general historical statement about tea adulteration. Keep it general, or add a source before you post it.
