# Post 10: A Bavarian country doctor watches the American Civil War

- **Type:** quick post.
- **Status:** all three quotes checked against the page images, including the report's date (verification log: `verification/quick.md`).
- **Image:** Rothenfels, `bsb00145146` canvas 67, crop of the lines from "und hat die Auswanderer Wuth" to "noch einmal in Deutschland!". Viewer: https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00145146?page=67

## Single post

July 1861, three months into the US Civil War. A Franconian doctor says the "emigrants' craze" has abated since "this praised Eldorado of freedom" is shaken by civil war. All "authenticated letters of lament," he adds, end: "Oh, if only we were once more in our beloved homeland!"

## Short thread

1. Rothenfels, Lower Franconia. The district doctor dates his report July 1861, about three months after the American Civil War began. He writes about the farmers of his district, from whom "whole trains formerly went, especially to North America."

2. Now the "emigrants' craze" has abated, "since this praised Eldorado of freedom is shaken to its core by civil war and the dollar spring of earnings etc. etc. is blocked."

3. Then he sums up, in his own words, the letters that came back: "Oh, if only we were once more in our beloved homeland! Once more in Germany! So end all the authenticated letters of lament from beyond the sea!"

4. Öttingen, Swabia, gives numbers: about 450 people left in 20 years, 350 of them for America. Most were Jews, servants and farming people: the first for free trade, the others for cheap land and high wages. Some saved money and came home.

5. Münnerstadt, Lower Franconia, counted one year exactly. In 1859, 27 people emigrated, 25 of them to America: 15 men and 12 women, 7 of them children.

6. Source: the 1858-1861 Bavarian district physicians' reports (Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, Cgm 6874), open data: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24523214

## Original German

- Rothenfels (`bsb00145146` canvas 66-67; date on canvas 3, "Rothenfels im Juli 1861"): "gerade von ihnen gingen aber früher ganze Züge besonders nach Nordamerika, und hat die Auswanderer Wuth dahin nachgelassen, seit dieß geprießene Eldorado der Freiheit durch Bürgerkrieg tiefinnerst erschüttert und des Verdienstes Dollarquelle ct. ct. verstopft wird; O! wären wir noch einmal in unserer geliebten Heimath! noch einmal in Deutschland! so schließen alle verbürgten Jammerbriefe von jenseits des Meeres!"
- Öttingen (`bsb00044079` canvas 47): "In den lezten 20. Jahren sind etwa 450 Personen aus unserem Bezirk ausgewandert, die meisten in den Jahren 1851–55. nemlich 260, darunter 350. nach Amerika; die große Mehrzahl der Ausgewanderten bestund in Juden, Dienstboten und Bauersleuten, erstere wegen der ungebundenen Handelsfreiheit in Amerika, leztere wegen leichterer Erwerbung von Grundbesitz und wegen der hohen Arbeitslöhne [...]"
- Münnerstadt (`bsb00145124` canvas 612): "Im Jahre 1859 sind 27 Personen ausgewandert, davon 25 nach Amerika und 2 in deutsche Bundesstaaten. Darunter waren 15 männliche und 12 weibliche Individuen [...]"

## Caveats

- Rothenfels does not quote a single letter. "So end all the letters" is his polemical summary.
- "Abated" translates his word *nachgelassen*.
- Öttingen: the ink reads "namely 260, among them 350 to America". Since 350 cannot be part of 260, the 350 must refer to the total of about 450. Use 450 and 350, and leave the 260 out. "About" marks the 450 as his estimate.
- Münnerstadt presents emigration as rare in his district. Do not present 27 as a large number.
