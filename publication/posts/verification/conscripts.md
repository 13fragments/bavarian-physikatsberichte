# Conscripts: check of the quoted passages against the page images

All readings below come from the page images, not from the machine text. Heights are written as feet ′, inches ″, and lines ‴. The arithmetic on the pages shows 12 lines to the inch (5′8″2‴ minus 5′6″5‴ is given as 1″9‴). Metric values are my conversions with the Bavarian foot of 29.19 cm and are not in the source.

### conscripts_erlangen_p66  (Erlangen Stadt, Mittelfranken; bsb00140610 canvas 66; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00140610?page=66)
- Claim supported: Yes. The 20-year mean height is 5′7″6‴, the tallest year-class is 1823, the shortest are 1832, 1833, and 1834, the German mean is taken as 5′6″, and the shares too short for 1830/37 are 2.3% (Erlangen), 2.85 (Mittelfranken), and 1.63 (Bavaria).
- German (ink): "Als durchschnittliches Maß finden wir bei den Altersklassen 18 19/28 5′10″7‴, für die nächsten Jahre 5′7″2‴ als Durchschnitt der 20 Jahre 5′7″6‴. Der größte Jahrgang war 1823 mit 5′8″2‴ die geringsten Jahrgänge die von 1832, 33 und 34 mit je 5′6″5‴, also größte Differenz 1″9‴. Da man für Deutschland im Allgemeinen als Mittelgröße der Männer 5′6″ annimmt, so sind die Erlanger also um etwas darüber hinaus." And: "Bei Betrachtung der Beilage 8 finden wir von 18 30/37 2,3 % der Conscribirten von Erlangen mindermäßig, für Mittelfranken 2,85, für Bayern 1,63."
- English: "As the average measure we find for the year-classes 1819–28 5′10″7‴, for the following years 5′7″2‴, as the average of the 20 years 5′7″6‴. The tallest year-class was 1823 with 5′8″2‴, the shortest year-classes those of 1832, 33 and 34 with 5′6″5‴ each, so the greatest difference is 1″9‴. Since for Germany in general 5′6″ is taken as the middle height of men, the men of Erlangen are thus somewhat above it." And: "Looking at Appendix 8, we find for 1830–37 2.3% of the conscripts of Erlangen under the minimum height, for Mittelfranken 2.85, for Bavaria 1.63."
- Corrections against the machine text: "18 14/28" -> "18 19/28" (two periods of ten year-classes, 1819–28 and 1829–38, which matches "20 Jahre"); "Dr. Ingely" -> "Dr. Angely"; "Dr. Friedrich" -> "Dr. Friedreich"; "zu tiefernden" -> "zu liefernden"; "von 18 43/57" -> "von 18 30/57"; "um 34" -> "und 34"; "5′ 6″" was right.
- Confidence: high (every word and digit read).
- Caveats:
  - Do not quote the first-period figure "5′10″7‴". The ink clearly says 5′10″7‴, but that value contradicts the same sentence: it is higher than the tallest single year (5′8″2‴), and it cannot average with 5′7″2‴ to 5′7″6‴. The physician most probably transposed 5′7″10‴ (then the mean of 5′7″10‴ and 5′7″2‴ is exactly 5′7″6‴). That is my inference, not the ink. The 20-year mean, the year-class extremes, and the 5′6″ are safe to quote.
  - "Year-class" (Altersklasse, Jahrgang) means birth year. The height table (Appendix 7) covers men born 1819 to 1838. It does not contain the men born in 1816 and 1817.
  - The physician does not name the foot on canvases 66 to 68, and a text search of the whole Erlangen report found no named foot. If it is the Bavarian foot, 5′7″6‴ is about 164.2 cm, 5′8″2‴ about 165.8 cm, 5′6″5‴ about 161.5 cm, and 5′6″ about 160.5 cm. The "German mean 5′6″" is a figure he takes from the literature ("man ... annimmt"); it may be in a different foot, so the comparison is his, not a measured fact.
  - The percentages for 1830/37 come from his Appendix 8, which he compiled from Hermann's printed statistics ("Herrmanns Beiträgen") for the conscription years 1830 to 1857. These are conscription years, not birth years.

### conscripts_erlangen_p68  (Erlangen Stadt, Mittelfranken; bsb00140610 canvas 68; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00140610?page=68)
- Claim supported: Partly. He says the 1816 and 1817 birth cohorts were not noticeably smaller and contained no undersized men. The claim that they "had more bodily defects" is FALSE: he says the number of bodily defects was just as little noticeably larger.
- German (ink): "Die Zahl der Conscribirten ist selbst von den 1816 und 1817 Gebornen nicht auffallend kleiner, und eben so wenig die Zahl der körperlichen Gebrechen auffallend größer. Mindermäßige sind gar keine dabei."
- English: "The number of conscripts, even of those born in 1816 and 1817, is not noticeably smaller, and just as little is the number of bodily defects noticeably larger. There are no undersized men among them at all."
- Corrections against the machine text: "eben soweitig" -> "eben so wenig" (this misreading hides the negation); "Kilb" -> "Kolb"; "Erndtrausfall" -> "Erndteausfall"; "Thüerungsgefahr" -> "Theuerungsgefahr"; "betrofft" -> "betrifft"; "abweich" -> "abwich".
- Confidence: high (every word read).
- Caveats:
  - His conclusion, exactly: in Erlangen the famine cohorts show none of the three expected effects. They were not fewer, not shorter, and not more often defective. He presents this as a confirmation of Dr. Escherich (Würzburg, 1852), who held that earnings from industry protect better against dearth than farm work: "Unsere Beilage 8 zeigt, daß hier Escherich Recht hat, wenn er im Verdienste durch Industrie eine größere Schutzwehr gegen Theuerungsgefahr als in der ökonomischen Arbeit findet."
  - So Erlangen is his counter-example to the general rule, which he quotes from the statistician Kolb just before: "Die Conscribirten aus Theuerungs und Nothjahren bleiben nicht nur der Menge nach unter der Mittelzahl, sondern sie sind auch im Durchschnitt weniger kräftig und was sich mit Zahlen erweisen läßt, kleiner indem verhältnißmäßig weit mehr von ihnen als sonst unter dem Normalmaße bleiben." ("The conscripts from years of dearth and want not only fall below the average in number; they are also on average less strong and, which can be proved with figures, shorter, since proportionally far more of them than usual stay below the standard measure.") This is Kolb's statement, not the Erlangen physician's finding.
  - The evidence is his Appendix 8, which I did not see. He gives no numbers for the 1816 and 1817 cohorts in the text. The wording is "not noticeably" (nicht auffallend), which is a judgment, not a measurement. The mean-height table (Appendix 7) starts with men born in 1819, so the report has no mean height for the famine cohorts; "no undersized men" is the only height statement about them.

### conscripts_uffenheim_p53  (Uffenheim, Mittelfranken; bsb00143120 canvas 53; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00143120?page=53)
- Claim supported: Yes. All six numbers are in the ink, and the physician explains the good result by the purely rural population.
- German (ink): "Dagegen stellt sich das Verhältniß hinsichtlich der Tauglichkeit zum Militärdienste weit günstiger, weil es nur ländliche Bevölkerung ist; von 10000 Konscribirten sind untauglich: wegen Mangels an Größe im Königreich 195, in Mittelfr. 229, im LG. Uffenh. 149 und wegen Gebrechen [im Königreich] 2182 [in Mittelfr.] 2535 [im LG. Uffenh.] 1287."
- English: "By contrast, the ratio for fitness for military service is far more favorable, because the population is only rural; of 10,000 conscripts there are unfit: for lack of height, in the kingdom 195, in Mittelfranken 229, in the Uffenheim district 149; and for defects, 2182, 2535, 1287."
- Corrections against the machine text: "Täglichkeit" -> "Tauglichkeit"; "Uffenb." -> "Uffenh."; "Wohlfahenheit" -> "Wohlhabenheit". All numbers were right.
- Confidence: high (every digit read; the bracketed words stand for ditto marks in the ink).
- Caveats: "Because it is only a rural population" is his explanation, not a tested cause. He restates the district figures as "Unter 100 Rekruten sind somit 1,5 wegen mangelnder Größe und 12,9 wegen Gebrechen dienstuntauglich", which agrees with 149 and 1287 per 10,000. He gives no source or period for the kingdom and Mittelfranken figures; the kingdom's 195 equals the 1.95% that the Erlangen report gives for Bavaria in 1838/51. The same paragraph says the district has fewer young men liable for conscription (92,5 per 10,000 inhabitants, against 98,4 in Mittelfranken and 99,5 in the whole country), which he attributes to low fertility.

### conscripts_amberg_p89  (Amberg Stadt, Oberpfalz; bsb00026308 canvas 89; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00026308?page=89)
- Claim supported: Yes. Of 581 men measured, 73 were over 6 feet, the tallest was 6′7″6‴ in 1855/56, and only 16 of the 581 did not reach the standard minimum measure of 5′4″.
- German (ink): "Von den Gemessenen, deren Summe im zehnjährigen Durchschnitte 581 ist, waren 73, welche über 6′ Maaß hatten. Das Maximum der Größe war im Jahre 1855/56. 6′ 7″ 6‴ und von 581 Gemessenen hatten nur 16 Conscribirte das Normal Maaß 5′ 4″ nicht."
- English: "Of the men measured, whose total in the ten-year average is 581, there were 73 who measured over 6′. The maximum height was 6′ 7″ 6‴ in the year 1855/56, and of 581 men measured only 16 conscripts did not have the standard measure of 5′ 4″."
- Corrections against the machine text: "Concribirte" -> "Conscribirte"; "untäglich" -> "untauglich"; "122. 129" -> "122" (one number is struck out and 122 is written in the margin). The numbers 581, 73, 16, 5′4″, 738 were right.
- Confidence: high (every digit read; the scan is a coarse black-and-white copy, but the digits are clear).
- Caveats:
  - What the "nur 16" sentence says: "Normal Maaß" is the required minimum height, not an average. So 16 of 581 (2.8%) were too short, and the list on the same page confirms it: "wegen Mangel des Maaßes 16". The sentence does not say that only 16 men were of normal height.
  - "Im zehnjährigen Durchschnitte 581" must mean the ten-year total, not a yearly average, although the wording says "average". The page continues "Von 738 Conscribirten waren im zehnjährigen Durchschnitte 122 untauglich, demnach der 6te Theil", and the listed causes add up to 122. Say "581 measured over ten years".
  - The years are given as "Altersklassen 1849/50 bis 1859/60 inclusive". The physician calls this ten years. These look like conscription years, not birth years.
  - If the Bavarian foot is meant (he does not name it), 6′ is about 175 cm, 6′7″6‴ about 193 cm, and 5′4″ about 156 cm. 73 of 581 is 12.6% over 6 feet; I computed that share. The same page says the men of Amberg average "5′ 8 bis 9″", which is his estimate.

## Summary

Claims that hold, with every digit confirmed in the ink:
- Erlangen: 20-year mean height 5′7″6‴; tallest year-class 1823 (5′8″2‴); shortest 1832 to 1834 (5′6″5‴ each); German mean taken as 5′6″; too short in 1830/37: Erlangen 2.3%, Mittelfranken 2.85, Bavaria 1.63.
- Uffenheim: per 10,000 conscripts, unfit for lack of height 195 / 229 / 149 and for defects 2182 / 2535 / 1287 (kingdom / Mittelfranken / Uffenheim); his explanation is the purely rural population.
- Pegnitz: 32.26 of 100 unfit, 5.16 for height and 27.10 for defects.
- Amberg: 73 of 581 over 6 feet; maximum 6′7″6‴ in 1855/56; 16 of 581 below the minimum measure of 5′4″.
- Neustadt an der Haardt: 449 liable, 66 unfit (24 and 42).

Claims that fail or need a change:
- FAILS: "the 1816 and 1817 cohorts had more bodily defects" (Erlangen, canvas 68). The ink says the opposite: the number of bodily defects was "eben so wenig ... auffallend größer" (just as little noticeably larger). The machine text had garbled "eben so wenig", which removed the negation.
- Needs care: Eschenbach "about one quarter unfit". The physician writes "der 4te Theil", but his counts are 31 of 166, which is 18.7%.
- Needs care: Erlangen first-period mean "5′10″7‴" is in the ink but is an evident slip (probably for 5′7″10‴). Do not quote it. Also correct the year-classes to 1819–28, not 1814–28.
- Needs care: Amberg "581 in the ten-year average" is a ten-year total. "Normal Maaß" means the minimum height.
- Not answered by the source: the Erlangen physician does not name the foot. The Bavarian foot is an assumption.

Strongest single hook: an Erlangen physician in 1860 tested the famine theory on his own town and found nothing. Kolb's rule said boys born in years of dearth would be fewer, weaker, and shorter. But of the men born in 1816 and 1817, "Mindermäßige sind gar keine dabei" (not one was under the minimum height), their number was not noticeably smaller, and their defects were not noticeably more. His explanation: wages from industry protected a town better than farm work did. The post must therefore present Erlangen as a null result with a 19th-century explanation, not as evidence of famine damage.
