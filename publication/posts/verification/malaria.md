### malaria_lauingen_p174  (Lauingen, Schwaben und Neuburg; bsb00009733, dataset canvas 174 = library image 87; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00009733?page=174)
- Claim supported: Yes, but for the village of Peterswörth: Danube floods and a pond in the village made it a rich source of intermittent fever, and the fever lost its force after the Danube ran deeper, the river area was regulated, and the pond dried out.
- German (ink): "Früher war es häufigen Ueberschwemmungen durch die Donau ausgesetzt, und wurde dadurch, sowie durch die Exhalationen eines im Orte selbst befindlichen Weihers eine ergiebige Quelle für Wechselfieber. Seit dem tiefern Gange der Donau und Regulirung des Stromgebietes, sowie seit Austrocknung des Weihers ist die Kraft der dort herrschenden Malaria gebrochen."
- English: "Formerly it was exposed to frequent floods from the Danube, and through these, and through the exhalations of a pond in the village itself, it became a rich source of intermittent fever. Since the Danube runs deeper and its river area was regulated, and since the pond dried out, the force of the malaria that prevails there is broken."
- Corrections against the machine text: `Peterswötho` -> `Peterswörth` (the subject "es" is this village); `Regulierung` -> `Regulirung`; `heerschenden` -> `herrschenden`.
- Confidence: high (every word read).
- Caveats: "es" is Peterswörth, a village of 364 people in 87 families "in a wide lowland with much moor ground", described in the sentences just before. Do not write "a pond inside the town of Lauingen". "Exhalationen" and "Malaria" (bad air) show the miasma theory of the time; the physician states cause and effect as his judgment and gives no numbers and no dates. "Austrocknung" does not say whether people drained the pond or it dried out by itself.

### malaria_lauingen_p69  (added page; Lauingen, Schwaben und Neuburg; bsb00009733, dataset canvases 67-70 = library images 34-35; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00009733?page=69)
- Claim supported: Yes, for the whole district: intermittent fever was formerly very common in the places on the Danube, and for several years it has been far less widespread, which the physician attributes mainly to the deeper Danube, river regulation, rarer floods, and the drying of backwaters, swamps, and ditches.
- German (ink): "In frühern Jahren waren im Frühjahre und Herbste in den an der Donau gelegenen Orten die Wechselfieber sehr häufig, welche meist als Tertian-Fieber auftraten. [...] Seit mehreren Jahren erscheinen aber diese Wechselfieber in weit geringerer Extensität, was vorzüglich dem auch seit einigen Jahren beobachteten tiefern Gange der Donau, der Flußregulirung, und den dadurch bedingten seltenern [?] Ueberschwemmungen, sowie dem natürlichen u. künstlichen Austrocknen von Altwässern, Sümpfen und Gräben zuzuschreiben ist."
- English: "In earlier years, in spring and autumn, intermittent fevers were very frequent in the places on the Danube, and they mostly appeared as tertian fever. [...] For several years, however, these intermittent fevers have appeared to a far smaller extent. This is to be attributed chiefly to the deeper course of the Danube, also observed for some years, to the river regulation and the rarer floods that result from it, and to the natural and artificial drying of backwaters, swamps, and ditches."
- Corrections against the machine text: `austraten` -> `auftraten`; `Petterswirth` -> `Peterswörth` (in the omitted part); `Gündelfingen` -> `Gundelfingen` (omitted part); `Ausfrocknen` -> `Austrocknen`; `Simpfen` -> `Sümpfen`; `tieferem` -> `tiefern`; `seltenen` -> `seltenern [?]`.
- Confidence: medium (one word marked [?]; "seltenern" or "seltenen", the meaning does not change).
- Caveats: This is the better quote for the post, because it covers the district and names "natural and artificial" drying. The first sentence ends library image 34; the rest is on image 35. The omitted part says the fevers arose from the "exhalations" of stagnant water left by floods, mostly on the right bank and at Peterswörth on the left bank, and that the Brenz river flooded low houses in Gundelfingen "and so became a source of fever". Cause and effect are the physician's judgment; no counts are given.

### malaria_germersheim_p84  (Germersheim, Pfalz; bsb00140616 canvases 83-84; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00140616?page=84)
- Claim supported: Yes, with the period added: in the civil hospital (Bürgerhospital) of Germersheim, in the three years 1858-1860 together, 142 of 667 patients had intermittent fever; the military hospital treated 10,197 intermittent-fever patients from October 1848 to the end of March 1858.
- German (ink): "[...] es betrug nemlich die Gesammtzahl in 3 Jahren 1858–60 1086 Spitalberechtigte, 667 Kranke und hierunter 142 Wechselfieberkranke. Im Militärkrankenhause stellte sich der Wechselfieberkrankenstand vom October 1848 bis Ende März 1858, wie folgt: [Tab. XXII] Summa von October 1848 bis Ende März 1858 in Behandlung gewesener Wechselfieberkranker 10,197"
- English: "[...] the total in the 3 years 1858–60 was 1086 persons entitled to hospital care, 667 patients, and among these 142 intermittent-fever patients. In the military hospital the number of intermittent-fever patients from October 1848 to the end of March 1858 was as follows: [Table XXII] Total of intermittent-fever patients treated from October 1848 to the end of March 1858: 10,197"
- Corrections against the machine text: yearly totals `885` -> `785` (1853/54) and first quarter 1857/58 `83` -> `85`; year labels `18 50/52` -> `1850/51` and `18 50/51` -> `1851/52` (both overwritten in ink); 1851/52 quarters `98` -> `78` and `609` -> `607`; `Besandlung gewehrter` -> `Behandlung gewesener`; quarter headings `Anittel` -> `Quartal`; grand total line `209,378` and `1:20,532` and "i. e. 20½" are correct. The machine also misread several numbers in the second and third lines of the cells; I did not use those.
- Confidence: high for the text sentences and for the yearly patient totals; the dense second and third lines of each cell are left out.
- Caveats:
  - The 667 and 142 are three-year totals, not one year. The previous page (canvas 83) gives the yearly averages in ink: "bei einer Zahl von 362 durch Abonnement Spitalberechtigter unter 222 Kranken 47 Wechselfieberkranke [...] oder auf 100 Kranke kommen jährlich 21,16 Wechselfieberkranke". So about one hospital patient in five. The physician says the numbers cover only people cared for inside the hospitals, not outpatients.
  - Table XXII, yearly patient totals read from the ink (fiscal year, October to September): 1848/49 1817; 1849/50 898; 1850/51 1515; 1851/52 1356; 1852/53 1656; 1853/54 785; 1854/55 963; 1855/56 336; 1856/57 770; 1857/58 (two quarters only) 85 + 16 = 101. These add up to 10,197, which matches the written total. Each row also adds up from its four quarters.
  - Under each patient count the table gives a second number and a ratio (total 209,378 and 1:20,532, "i. e. 20½"). The table does not label them. The arithmetic (209,378 / 10,197 = 20.53) shows they are days of care and days per patient. This is my inference; say "about 20½ days per patient" only with that reservation.
  - The table does not continue on canvas 85. Canvas 85 holds different tables (XXIII and XXIV, civil hospital 1854-1860 by sex, fever type, and month). I did not check canvas 85 against the image.
  - The table gives no garrison size, so no rate per soldier can be stated. Germersheim was a fortress on the Rhine, not on the Danube.

### malaria_kastl_p86  (Kastl, Oberpfalz; bsb00020843 canvases 86-87; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00020843?page=86)
- Claim supported: Mostly. The fevers appear in spring and the summer months, and especially also in places that have cistern water; most are simple or double tertian, the quotidian type is rare, and the quartan type is rarer still. The ink does not say that people drink the cistern water.
- German (ink): "Die Wechselfieber tauchen im Frühlinge und in den Sommermonaten auf und insbesonders auch an solchen Orten, die Cisternenwasser haben. Die Wechselfieber haben in der Mehrzahl den einfachen oder doppelten Tertiantypus, selten ist der Quotidian und noch seltener der Quartan-Typus."
- English: "The intermittent fevers appear in spring and in the summer months, and especially also in such places as have cistern water. Most of the intermittent fevers have the simple or double tertian type; the quotidian type is rare and the quartan type rarer still."
- Corrections against the machine text: `Tertiantypos` -> `Tertiantypus`; `Quartan-Typos` -> `Quartan-Typus`. On the next page: `An das Intermittens` -> `Da das Intermittens`; `Büchen` -> `Bächen`.
- Confidence: high (every word read).
- Caveats: Write "places that have cistern water", not "where people drink cistern water". The physician's explanation follows on canvas 87 and is a miasma theory: because the fever occurs "nur in den mit stagnirenden Gewässern durch Austritt von Bächen versehenen Niederungen" after rainy weather, "so scheint eine Intermittens-Miasma-Bildung durch ausdünstende, stagnirende Gewässer [...] verursacht zu werden, was sich auch an den Orten erklären läßt, die Cisternenwasser haben". He also says the course is "im Ganzen günstig" (on the whole favorable). "Double tertian" means two overlapping tertian cycles, which gives a daily attack.

### malaria_monheim_p67  (Monheim, Schwaben und Neuburg; bsb00094713 canvases 66-67; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00094713?page=67)
- Claim supported: Yes. Floods, mainly of the Danube, throw out plant and animal matter that decays; this produces "the so-called malaria" and intermittent fever, which has become endemic.
- German (ink): "Durch diese Überschwemmungen vorzüglich der Donau werden sowohl vegetabilische als animalische Stoffe ausgeworfen, dem Fäulnißprocesse unterworfen, dadurch die sogenannte Malaria und die daraus entstehenden Krankheiten, besonders Wechselfieber erzeugt. Letzteres namentlich zeigt sich unter allen Formen, im Allgemeinen gutartigen Charakters, ist endemisch geworden und erscheint oft als Epidemie, vorzugsweise im Frühlinge, Sommer und Herbste, übersteigt aber höchst selten die westlich und nordwestlich gelegenen Höhen."
- English: "Through these floods, chiefly of the Danube, both vegetable and animal matter is thrown out and subjected to the process of decay, and this produces the so-called malaria and the diseases that arise from it, especially intermittent fever. The latter in particular shows itself in all forms, in general of a benign character, has become endemic and often appears as an epidemic, mainly in spring, summer, and autumn, but very rarely climbs above the heights that lie to the west and northwest."
- Corrections against the machine text: none in these sentences.
- Confidence: high (every word read).
- Caveats: The sentence starts at the bottom of canvas 66 ("Durch diese Überschwemmungen vorzüglich der Donau werden sowohl vegetabilische") and continues on canvas 67. Here "Malaria" means bad air, a cause of disease, not the disease itself. This is the plainest statement of the miasma theory in the set. The physician calls the fever generally benign.

### malaria_guenzburg_p231  (Günzburg, Schwaben und Neuburg; bsb00144874 canvas 231; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00144874?page=231)
- Claim supported: Yes. The physician treats intermittent fever as a predominantly endemic disease of his district, and defines that as a disease that shows itself every year to a greater or lesser extent.
- German (ink): "Wenn aber bei uns das Wechselfieber die Bedeutung einer vorherrschend endemischen Krankheit d. h. einer Krankheit, die sich alljährlich in größerer oder geringerer Verbreitung zeigt, so müssen [?] bei uns ständige örtliche Verhältnisse gegeben sein, ein Umstand, der bei Aufsuchung der ätiologischen Verhältnisse nicht außer Acht gelassen werden darf."
- English: "But if intermittent fever among us [has] the significance of a predominantly endemic disease, that is, a disease that shows itself every year to a greater or lesser extent, then permanent local conditions must exist among us, a circumstance that must not be left out of account in the search for the causes."
- Corrections against the machine text: `soll-jährlich` -> `alljährlich` ("sich" is written above the line); `größeren oder geringeren` -> `größerer oder geringerer`; `müßten` -> `müssen [?]`; `stärkere örtliche` -> `ständige örtliche`; `Verholtnisse` -> `Verhältnisse`. Earlier on the page: `samma samarum` -> `summa summarum`; `Medizostereien` -> `Medikastereien`; `Wechselfieber-Spüter` -> `Wechselfieber-Spucke` (spook).
- Confidence: medium (one word marked [?]; "müssen" or "müßten", the meaning barely changes).
- Caveats: The physician left out the verb of the "Wenn" clause (a "hat" is missing after "zeigt"); the sense is clear, and the translation supplies "[has]". ### malaria_riedenburg_p104  (Riedenburg, Oberpfalz; bsb00146476 canvas 104; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00146476?page=104)
- Claim supported: Yes. Number 49 in the list of plant remedies of the people is willow bark for intermittent fever.
- German (ink): "49. Weidenrinde gut für Wechselfieber."
- English: "49. Willow bark, good for intermittent fever."

## Summary

**Claims that hold.**
- River regulation and drying were followed by less fever: holds for the Lauingen district, in the physician's own words, twice (Peterswörth village on library image 87, viewer page 174, and the whole Danube side of the district on library image 35, viewer pages 67-70). It is his judgment, without numbers.
- Germersheim hospital counts: hold. 142 of 667 civil hospital patients (three years, 1858-1860); 10,197 military hospital cases from October 1848 to March 1858, with yearly totals that add up.
- Kastl seasons and fever types: hold.
- Monheim "die sogenannte Malaria" and "endemisch geworden": hold.
- Günzburg as an endemic district: holds.
- Karlstadt rarity and returning soldiers: holds exactly.
- Riedenburg willow bark, remedy 49: holds.

**Claims that fail or need a change.**
- "A pond inside the town [of Lauingen]": fails. The pond was in the village of Peterswörth.
- The Lauingen viewer link `?page=174`: holds. (The verifier first judged it "fails" and named page 87; the editor's correction at the top of this log explains why `?page=174` is right.) `bsb00009733_0175` contains nothing on fever.
- Günzburg "yearly deaths, 1851/52 to 1857/58": needs a change. The table has six years and omits 1856/57; total 15 deaths.
- Kastl "where people drink cistern water": needs a change. The ink says "places that have cistern water".
- Germersheim "142 among 667": true only as a three-year total; state the period. Tab. XXII does not continue on canvas 85.
- Monheim page 66 shows smaller swamps after the Danube correction, but Monheim does not report less fever. The retreat claim rests on Lauingen alone among the checked pages.

**Strongest hook.** Lauingen, 1860: "Seit dem tiefern Gange der Donau und Regulirung des Stromgebietes, sowie seit Austrocknung des Weihers ist die Kraft der dort herrschenden Malaria gebrochen." A Bavarian district physician records the end of malaria in a Danube village decades before anyone knew that mosquitoes carry it, and he credits the right measure for the wrong reason (bad air from standing water). Pair it with the Germersheim fortress hospital on the Rhine: 10,197 intermittent-fever cases in nine and a half years.
