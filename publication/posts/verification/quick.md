# Verification of quotes for the three short posts (beer as food, fake tea, emigration)

I read each passage on the page image of the Bayerische Staatsbibliothek scan, in enlarged crops. The crops are in `scratchpad/crops/quick/`. The machine transcription served only to find the lines. All six passages hold. One number in the Öttingen passage is ambiguous in the ink itself, and I explain it there.

### quick_beer_oberdorf_p31-32  (Oberdorf, Schwaben und Neuburg; bsb00146468 canvas 31 to 32; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00146468?page=32)
- Claim supported: On working days the usual drink is water, and men seldom drink beer other than white beer, even during the hardest field work; on Sundays and holidays they drink 6 to 8 Maß in the inn "without harm"; brandy is rare.
- German (ink): "Getränk ist in der Regel das Wasser, Bier wird selbst von den Männern an Arbeitstagen, selbst bei der strengsten Feldarbeit außer dem weißen Biere selten getrunken, dagegen sprechen sie an Sonn, u. Feyertagen dem Maaßkruge im Wirthshause fleißig zu, sodaß ein Mann 6–8 Maaß ohne Schaden trinkt. Der Genuß des Brandtweins ist selten, es wird wohl bei großer Kälte manchmal ein Gläschen verschluckt, oder zum weißen Biere macht ein halbes Schoppenglas unter 6–8 Männern die Runde."
- English: "The drink is as a rule water. Even the men seldom drink beer on working days, even during the hardest field work, except for white beer; on Sundays and holidays, however, they apply themselves diligently to the Maß mug in the inn, so that a man drinks 6 to 8 Maß without harm. The use of brandy is rare; in great cold a small glass is sometimes swallowed, or with the white beer half a Schoppen glass goes the round among 6 to 8 men."
- Corrections against the machine text: "Maaßtruge" -> "Maaßkruge"; "die Kunde" -> "die Runde"; "auf-ser" -> "außer" (the ink splits the word at the line end as "auß-ser"; I give the joined word in its normal form).
- Confidence: high (every word read).
- Caveats: The sentence starts on canvas 31 and ends on canvas 32, so cite both. "Without harm" is the physician's judgment, not a measurement. The exception "außer dem weißen Biere" matters: the men do drink white (wheat) beer on working days; the 6 to 8 Maß on Sundays refers to the inn, and the ink does not name the beer type there. A Bavarian Maß of the time was about 1.07 litres, so 6 to 8 Maß is roughly 6.4 to 8.6 litres; this conversion is mine, not the physician's. The brandy share is tiny: half a Schoppen glass shared by 6 to 8 men. The machine text of canvas 31 also says that workers take bread and white beer to the field in the morning; I did not check those upper lines in the ink.

### quick_beer_passau_p79  (Passau Stadt, Niederbayern; bsb00113404 canvas 79; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00113404?page=79)
- Claim supported: Supper is usually soup and a few boiled potatoes with butter, and people often make do with only beer and bread; at breakfast the family members drink coffee, while the servants and journeymen get milk soup or another soup.
- German (ink): "Das Nachtmahl besteht gewöhnlich nur aus einer Suppe und einigen gesottenen Kartoffeln mit Butter. Häufig begnügt man sich auch nur mit Bier und Brod. Zum Frühstück wird von den Familienangehörigen Kaffee, von dem Hausgesinde und den Handwerksgesellen Milch- oder eine andere Suppe genommen."
- English: "Supper usually consists only of a soup and a few boiled potatoes with butter. Often people also content themselves with only beer and bread. For breakfast the members of the family take coffee; the household servants and the journeymen take milk soup or another soup."
- Corrections against the machine text: "Cartoffeln" -> "Kartoffeln" (the initial has the same form as the K of "Kaffee"); "Haus gesinde" -> "Hausgesinde".
- Confidence: high (every word read).
- Caveats: "Only beer and bread" describes supper (Nachtmahl), not the whole day's food. According to the machine text of the page before (canvas 78, not checked in the ink), the subject is the burgher class in general ("Die Bürgerklasse im Allgemeinen"), not the poor. The next sentence on this page says that the poorer class as a rule goes without breakfast.

### quick_tea_klingenberg_p101  (Klingenberg, Unterfranken und Aschaffenburg; bsb00145112 canvas 101; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00145112?page=101)
- Claim supported: Blackberry (bramble) leaves are traded and go in great quantity to North America, "probably" to adulterate green tea.
- German (ink): "Die Wachholderbeere kömmt in den Handel; ebenso die Blätter der Brombeere, die in großer Quantität nach Nordamerika, wahrscheinlich zur Fälschung des grünen Thees, gehen"
- English: "The juniper berry is traded; so are the leaves of the blackberry, which go in great quantity to North America, probably for the adulteration of green tea."
- Corrections against the machine text: "Brombeer" -> "Brombeere" (the last letter runs into the column border); otherwise none in this sentence.
- Confidence: high (every word read; "wahrscheinlich" and "Brombeere" are clear).
- Caveats: The plant is the blackberry (Brombeere, Rubus), which "bramble" translates correctly; it is not raspberry or bilberry, both of which the same entry names separately as plentiful. The purpose is the physician's guess: he writes "wahrscheinlich". He gives no quantity, buyer, or source. The text stands in a table cell, in the column on medicinal plants and vegetation. The sentence continues after "gehen," with a remark on the narrow scope of medicinal botany, so end the quote there without a full stop or add "[...]".

### quick_emigration_rothenfels_p67  (Rothenfels, Unterfranken und Aschaffenburg; bsb00145146 canvas 66 to 67; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00145146?page=67)
- Claim supported: Whole groups of farmers used to emigrate, especially to North America, and the "emigration craze" has abated since that praised "Eldorado of freedom" was shaken by civil war.
- German (ink): "gerade von ihnen gingen aber früher ganze Züge besonders nach Nordamerika, und hat die Auswanderer Wuth dahin nachgelassen, seit dieß geprießene Eldorado der Freiheit durch Bürgerkrieg tiefinnerst erschüttert und des Verdienstes Dollarquelle ct. ct. verstopft wird; O! wären wir noch einmal in unserer geliebten Heimath! noch einmal in Deutschland! so schließen alle verbürgten Jammerbriefe von jenseits des Meeres!"
- English: "but it was precisely from among them that whole trains formerly went, especially to North America, and the emigrants' craze for that place has abated since this praised Eldorado of freedom is shaken to its core by civil war and the dollar spring of earnings etc. etc. is blocked. 'Oh, if only we were once more in our beloved homeland! Once more in Germany!' So end all the authenticated letters of lament from beyond the sea!"
- Corrections against the machine text: "nach galaschen" -> "nachgelassen"; "Oldorado" -> "Eldorado"; "Dollar quella et. et." -> "Dollarquelle ct. ct."
- Confidence: high (every word read).
- Caveats: "Them" ("ihnen") refers to "die ausschließlich Ackerbau Treibenden", those who live only from farming, named in the clause before; that clause begins on canvas 66 ("Gewöhnlich behar-") and says that they usually stay at the parental hearth. The physician writes "nachgelassen" (abated), so "cooled" is a fair paraphrase but not his word. Date: the first page of the report (canvas 3, read in the ink) is dated "Rothenfels im Juli 1861" and carries the receipt note "pr 31/7.61". The American Civil War began in April 1861. So the physician wrote this about three months after the war began, and this report is from 1861, not 1860. The verbs are in the present tense ("erschüttert ... wird"). The homesick letters are his summary; he quotes no single letter, and the tone is polemical.

### quick_emigration_muennerstadt_p612  (Münnerstadt, Unterfranken und Aschaffenburg; bsb00145124 canvas 612; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00145124?page=612)
- Claim supported: In 1859, 27 persons emigrated, 25 to America and 2 to German federal states; 15 were male and 12 female; 7 were children up to 16 years, 18 were aged 16 to 40, and 2 were older.
- German (ink): "Im Jahre 1859 sind 27 Personen ausgewandert, davon 25 nach Amerika und 2 in deutsche Bundesstaaten. Darunter waren 15 männliche und 12 weibliche Individuen – dann Kinder bis zu 7 Jahren 3, und bis zu 16 Jahren 4 – also zusammen 7. – Im Alter von 16 bis 40 Jahren 18, darüber 2. Das Verlassen der Heimath geschieht selbst von Dienstboten nur selten."
- English: "In the year 1859, 27 persons emigrated, 25 of them to America and 2 to German federal states. Among them were 15 male and 12 female individuals; then children up to 7 years 3, and up to 16 years 4, so 7 together. Aged 16 to 40 years 18, above that 2. Leaving the homeland happens only rarely, even among servants."
- Corrections against the machine text: "Bundesstraßen" -> "Bundesstaaten"; "mannliche" -> "männliche". All numbers in the machine text are correct.
- Confidence: high (every word and digit read).
- Caveats: The numbers are consistent: 25 + 2 = 27, 15 + 12 = 27, and 3 + 4 + 18 + 2 = 27. The figure is for one year only. The physician's point is that emigration is rare here; the section heading is "Verharren in der Heimath und ihrem Leben" (staying in the homeland and its way of life). Do not present 27 as a large number.

### quick_emigration_oettingen_p47  (Öttingen, Schwaben und Neuburg; bsb00044079 canvas 47; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00044079?page=47)
- Claim supported: About 450 persons emigrated from the district in the last 20 years, most of them in 1851 to 1855; the great majority were Jews, servants, and farming people, and the physician gives a reason for each group.
- German (ink): "In den lezten 20. Jahren sind etwa 450 Personen aus unserem Bezirk ausgewandert, die meisten in den Jahren 1851–55. nemlich 260, darunter 350. nach Amerika; die große Mehrzahl der Ausgewanderten bestund in Juden, Dienstboten und Bauersleuten, erstere wegen der ungebundenen Handelsfreiheit in Amerika, leztere wegen leichterer Erwerbung von Grundbesitz und wegen der hohen Arbeitslöhne, was leicht Ersparungen und Erwerbung von Vermögen möglich macht, das nicht selten zur ersehnten Rükkehr in die Heimath benuzt wird."
- English: "In the last 20 years about 450 persons have emigrated from our district, most of them in the years 1851 to 1855, namely 260; among them 350 to America. The great majority of the emigrants consisted of Jews, servants, and farming people, the first because of the unrestricted freedom of trade in America, the latter because land is easier to acquire and because of the high wages, which easily allow savings and the acquisition of a fortune that is not seldom used for the longed-for return to the homeland."
- Corrections against the machine text: "benußt" -> "benuzt"; the machine's dashes around "260" are a full stop and a comma in the ink. The numbers are not garbled: the ink really reads 20, 450, 1851–55, 260, and 350.
- Confidence: high for the reading (every word and digit read); see the caveat on what the numbers mean.
- Caveats: The ink says "nemlich 260, darunter 350. nach Amerika". Because 350 cannot be a part of 260, "darunter" must refer to the total of about 450. The safe reading is: about 450 emigrants in 20 years, 260 of them in 1851 to 1855, and 350 of the 450 to America. The physician's grammar leaves this to the reader, so a post should state the three numbers in separate clauses or omit the 260. "Etwa" marks 450 as his estimate. "Erstere" (the first) is the Jews; "leztere" (the latter) most naturally covers the servants and farming people. The return to the homeland with savings is his observation, without numbers. I did not check the date of this report; "the last 20 years" counts back from about 1860.

## Summary

All six claims hold in the ink. No claim fails.

- Oberdorf: holds, and it needs both canvases 31 and 32. Water is the working-day drink, with white beer as the stated exception; 6 to 8 Maß "without harm" is for Sundays and holidays in the inn.
- Passau: holds. "Only beer and bread" is about supper in the burgher class.
- Klingenberg: holds. The leaves are blackberry leaves, and "wahrscheinlich" marks the tea adulteration as the physician's guess.
- Rothenfels: holds, and the wording is stronger than the paraphrase. The report is dated July 1861, about three months after the American Civil War began.
- Münnerstadt: holds, with all numbers confirmed and consistent. The physician's point is that emigration is rare.
- Öttingen: holds. The numbers 260 and 350 are what the ink says; 350 can only be a part of the 450 total, not of the 260.

Strongest single hook: the Rothenfels physician, writing in July 1861, reports that the "Auswanderer Wuth" for North America has abated "seit dieß geprießene Eldorado der Freiheit durch Bürgerkrieg tiefinnerst erschüttert [...] wird". A Bavarian country doctor records the effect of the American Civil War on his farmers within three months of its start. For the beer post, the Oberdorf line "sodaß ein Mann 6–8 Maaß ohne Schaden trinkt" is the best hook, if the post also says that water was the working-day drink.
