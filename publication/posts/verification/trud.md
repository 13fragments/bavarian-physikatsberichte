# Verification of the *Trud* passages (angle `trud`)

I read every passage below on the page image, in enlarged crops. The crops are in `crops/trud/` in the scratchpad. All statements are folk beliefs that the district physicians report; none is the physician's own medical claim. The dates of the reports are 1858-1861.

Two claims in the brief do not match the ink. First, the Burgau physician does not say that the horses sweat: he says they have foam at the mouth, and tail and mane are tousled. Second, the Lauingen passage is about a *Hexe* (witch) who chokes the sleeper; the physician uses "Drude oder Hexe" as one being, and the pillow is thrown on the floor.

### trud_eschenbach_p53  (Eschenbach, Oberpfalz; bsb00072130 canvas 53; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00072130?page=53)
- Claim supported: Yes. The prevailing opinion attributes "Trud-Drücken", which the physician himself glosses as "Alpdrücken" (nightmare pressure), to witches.
- German (ink): "Das sogenannte Trud-Drücken (Alpdrücken) rührt nach der herrschenden Meinung ebenfalls von Hexen her."
- English: "The so-called Trud-pressing (nightmare pressure) also comes from witches, according to the prevailing opinion."
- Corrections against the machine text: "Alzdrücken" -> "Alpdrücken"; "Hezen" -> "Hexen".
- Confidence: high (every word read)
- Caveats: The paragraph is this one sentence; it is the first paragraph on the page. "Trud-Drücken" is underlined in the ink. "ebenfalls" (also) points back to the witch beliefs on canvas 52; I know canvas 52 only from the machine text, not from the image. The next paragraph is about a different subject (the Wild Hunt). The physician reports the belief; he does not share it.

### trud_burgau_p167  (Burgau, Schwaben; bsb00140602 canvas 167; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00140602?page=167)
- Claim supported: Yes. Belief in witches and Truden is the leading superstition, and even people who count themselves among the more educated classes confess it publicly.
- German (ink): "Vor Allem herrscht auch hier wie anderwärts der Glaube an Hexen und Truden (Druden) und selbst die zu den gebildeteren Classen sich Rechnenden schämen sich nicht, sich zu diesem Glauben öffentlich zu bekennen."
- English: "Above all, here as elsewhere, the belief in witches and Truden (Druden) prevails, and even those who count themselves among the more educated classes are not ashamed to profess this belief in public."
- Corrections against the machine text: "Vor Allend herrscht auf hier eine anderwärts" -> "Vor Allem herrscht auch hier wie anderwärts"; "Gepane" -> "Hexen"; "zu dem" -> "zu den"; "bekommen" -> "bekennen".
- Confidence: medium. The scan is small (1717 pixels wide) and the hand is hasty. I read every word, but "auch hier wie" rests on context as much as on letter shapes.
- Caveats: The sentence names witches and Truden together, not Truden alone. The physician says "those who count themselves" educated, which is a slight irony; do not shorten it to "the educated". The section heading is "Hang zum Aberglauben". The report is dated 1861 in its running title.

### trud_burgau_p168  (Burgau, Schwaben; bsb00140602 canvas 168; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00140602?page=168)
- Claim supported: Partly. The power of the Truden is inborn; they ride, press, and frighten people and cattle in their sleep; the horse is found exhausted in the morning with tousled tail and mane. The ink does NOT say the horse sweats. It says "Schaum vor dem Munde" (foam at the mouth), and "Schweif" is the tail, not sweat.
- German (ink): "Die Macht der Truden ist denselben angeboren, und besteht darin, Menschen und Vieh im Schlaf zu reiten, zu drücken, zu ängstigen, theils aus Wohllust, theils aus Haß; von den Thieren reiten sie zumal das Roß, das man dann am Morgen ganz matt und erschöpft, mit Schaum vor dem Munde, Schweif und Mähnen verzaust [?] und verwirrt, im Stalle findet."
- English: "The power of the Truden is inborn in them, and consists in riding, pressing, and frightening people and cattle in their sleep, partly from lust, partly from hatred; among the animals they ride the horse above all, which one then finds in the stable in the morning quite faint and exhausted, with foam at its mouth, tail and mane tousled and tangled."
- Corrections against the machine text: "Die Nacht der Truden" -> "Die Macht der Truden"; "ängtigen" -> "ängstigen"; "Wohllut" -> "Wohllust"; "mit Schauen vor dem Munde" -> "mit Schaum vor dem Munde"; "verzurst" -> "verzaust"; "Stalte" -> "Stalle".
- Confidence: medium. "verzaust" is one doubtful word; the scan is small.
- Caveats: The next sentence is also quotable and I read it with the same confidence: "Die Truden sind der Rekrutirungsfond für die Hexen; aus jüngeren Truden werden gern alte Hexen." ("The Truden are the recruiting pool for the witches; younger Truden readily become old witches."). All of this is stated as popular belief.

### trud_wegscheid_p96  (Wegscheid, Niederbayern; bsb00136007 canvas 96; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00136007?page=96)
- Claim supported: Yes. If a child's breasts swell, the Trud has suckled on them; the remedy is to tie a mirror and nine kinds of dung into the swaddling cloth.
- German (ink): "Der Glaube an Truden ist noch nicht erloschen z. B. Schwellen einem Kinde die Brüste an, so hat die Trud daran gesäugt; um abzuhelfen bindet man einen Spiege[l] u. neunerlei Koth /: von Vögeln u. Säugethieren :/ in die Windl."
- English: "The belief in Truden is not yet extinct, for example: if a child's breasts swell, the Trud has suckled on them; to remedy this, one ties a mirror and nine kinds of dung (from birds and mammals) into the swaddling cloth."
- Corrections against the machine text: none in substance. The machine text also has "Spiege"; the last letter of "Spiegel" is lost in the binding gutter at the line end.
- Confidence: medium, only because of the cut letter in "Spiege[l]". All other words are clear.
- Caveats: The physician says "Kinde" (child), not "newborn", and gives no medical explanation. The link to the harmless hormonal breast swelling of newborns is a modern interpretation and the post must mark it as such. The swaddling cloth ("Windl") does show that an infant is meant.

### trud_wegscheid_p97  (Wegscheid, Niederbayern; bsb00136007 canvas 97; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00136007?page=97)
- Claim supported: Yes. A "Trudenkreuz" cut into the bedstead above the head protects against the Trud.
- German (ink): "Ein Trudenkreuz, das man in die Bettlade, über dem Kopfe, einschneidet, schützt vor der Trud."
- English: "A Trud cross, which one cuts into the bedstead above the head, protects against the Trud."
- Corrections against the machine text: a comma after "Kopfe" is in the ink; otherwise none.
- Confidence: high (every word read)
- Caveats: The physician does not describe or draw the shape of the "Trudenkreuz". This sentence follows the canvas 96 passage directly, across the page break.

### trud_koetzting_p116  (Kötzting, Niederbayern; bsb00145113 canvas 116; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00145113?page=116)
- Claim supported: Yes. People still put "Druden-Füße" on doors, windows, and bedsteads, and say a special short prayer against the Druden. The physician draws the sign in the line.
- German (ink): "[...] es besteht hier noch heut zu Tage sehr die Furcht vor den Druden, und der Aberglaube setzt, um sich vor ihren schädlichen Einflüßen zu wahren, heute noch die so genannten Druden-Füße [drawn sign] an Thüren, Fenster und Bettstellen, und betet auch sein Stoß-Gebet eigener Art gegen diese Unholdinen."
- English: "[...] here the fear of the Druden is still very much alive today, and superstition, to guard against their harmful influences, still today sets the so-called Druden feet [drawn sign] on doors, windows, and bedsteads, and also says its own kind of quick prayer against these fiends."
- Corrections against the machine text: "Furcht vor den Druiden" -> "Furcht vor den Druden" (no i-dot in the ink, unlike "Druiden" three lines above); "zu wahrer" -> "zu wahren"; the characters "١٢" are the machine's rendering of the drawn sign; "Beltstellen" -> "Bettstellen"; "Unsoldinen" -> "Unholdinen".
- Confidence: high for the text (every word read).
- Caveats: The drawn sign is very small, about the height of one letter. It is a figure of straight crossing strokes, tall and narrow, with a point at the top and a base line, consistent with a pentagram drawn in one movement. It is too small and hasty to count five points with certainty, so write "a small star-like sign" or "a hastily drawn pentagram", not "a clear five-pointed star". Immediately before, the physician speculates that Celtic Druids ("die keltischen Druiden") once were active here, and so links "Druden" to "Druiden". That is his own etymology and it is wrong by modern scholarship; do not repeat it as fact.

### trud_dingolfing_p77  (Dingolfing, Niederbayern; bsb00049260 canvas 77; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00049260?page=77)
- Claim supported: Yes. Being a Trud is inborn, being a witch is acquired; both are believed unshakeably, and help against them is sought in the monasteries.
- German (ink): "Truden und Hexen, ersteres angeboren, letztere[s] [?] erworben, werden unumstößlich geglaubt, und Hilfe dagegen in den Klöstern gesucht."
- English: "Truden and witches, the former inborn, the latter acquired, are believed unshakeably, and help against them is sought in the monasteries."
- Corrections against the machine text: "Blöstern" -> "Klöstern". The machine reading "ersteres" is right. The ending of "letztere[s]" runs into the gutter shadow, so I cannot see whether a final "s" is there.
- Confidence: medium (one word ending marked [?]; it does not change the meaning)
- Caveats: "Truden are born, witches are made" is a fair paraphrase but not a literal quote. The monastery detail is a good addition for the post. The sentence before it reads: "Ahnungen und Träume sind in Jedermans Mund."

### trud_lauingen_p271  (Lauingen, Schwaben; bsb00009733 canvas 271; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00009733?page=271)
- Claim supported: Partly. Whoever seizes the "Drude oder Hexe" and does not let go sees her turn into a straw (Strohhalm). But the being that sits on the pillow is called "Hexe", the pillow is thrown on the floor first, and the attack is choking ("gewürgt"), not pressing.
- German (ink): "Wenn man Nachts von einer Hexe gewürgt wird, so soll man sogleich das Kopfkissen aus dem Bette auf den Boden der Schlafkamer hinauswerfen; da bleibt dann die Hexe auf dem Kissen sitzen und man kann sie beim Erwachen am Morgen erkennen und sehen; – faßt man die Drude oder Hexe mit den Händen, und laßt sie nicht mehr los, so verwandelt sie sich in einen Strohhalm, den man ja nicht aus der Hand lassen soll."
- English: "If a witch chokes you at night, you should at once throw the pillow out of the bed onto the floor of the bedroom; the witch then stays sitting on the pillow, and on waking in the morning you can recognize and see her; – if you seize the Drude or witch with your hands and do not let her go, she turns into a straw, which you must on no account let out of your hand."
- Corrections against the machine text: "Heve" -> "Hexe" (every time); "gewürzt" -> "gewürgt"; "so gleich" -> "sogleich"; "Ewachen" -> "Erwachen".
- Confidence: high (every word read)
- Caveats: This is item 31 in a numbered list of superstitions. The continuation, also read: "Wirft man in der Frühe den Strohhalm ins Feuer, so muß die Hexe erscheinen; auch kann man einen solchen Strohhalm an eine Thüre annageln oder man kann den Strohhalm tüchtig auspeitschen." (Throw the straw into the fire in the morning and the witch must appear; or nail it to a door, or whip it soundly.) Then the physician says people soon hear that some woman fell ill that night and cannot leave her bed, and "dieß ist dann die Hexe". The post should say "Drude or witch", because the physician treats the two words as the same being here.

### trud_parsberg_p129  (Parsberg, Oberpfalz; bsb00003359 canvas 129; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00003359?page=129)
- Claim supported: Yes. A woman in childbed can protect herself from the Trud by putting on a piece of her husband's clothing.
- German (ink): "Auch dadurch kann sich eine Wöchnerin vor [?] der Trude sichern, daß sie ein Kleidungsstück von ihrem Manne anzieht."
- English: "A woman in childbed can also protect herself from the Trude by putting on a piece of clothing of her husband's."
- Corrections against the machine text: "Wöchserin" -> "Wöchnerin"; "von der Trude" -> "vor [?] der Trude"; "Kleidungstück" -> "Kleidungsstück".
- Confidence: medium (one word marked [?]; "vor" or "von", the meaning is the same). The scan is a high-contrast microfilm with slanted lines.
- Caveats: The same page gives a second story that I also read: a woman in childbed was tormented by the Trud so badly that her husband found her "wie in Schweiße gebadet ohnmächtig"; the Trud had power only because the husband had named a time for his return ("in einer halben Stunde komm ich wieder") and did not keep it. The page also says the bed curtain protects women in childbed from all attacks of evil. All of this is reported belief.

### trud_parsberg_p131  (Parsberg, Oberpfalz; bsb00003359 canvas 131; https://www.digitale-sammlungen.de/de/view/bsb00003359?page=131)
- Claim supported: Yes. A ritual call forces the Trud to appear in person the next day, and the Truden found in this way "were always old women". The same passage is a close description of sleep paralysis: a weight like a hundredweight, and the victim cannot speak a word.
- German (ink), symptom: "Man versichert, daß es dann unmöglich sey, ein Wort zu sprechen. Wenn ein anderer einen solchen Gequälten anspricht, wird er Augenblicklich von seiner Last befreit."
- German (ink), ritual: "Schreit man dann gleich nach: „In drei Teufelsnamen kom morgen“, so muß sich die Trude den andern Tag in Person stellen. Auf diese Weise will man schon viele Truden entdeckt haben, die immer alte Weiber waren."
- English, symptom: "People affirm that it is then impossible to speak a word. When another person speaks to someone so tormented, he is instantly freed of his burden."
- English, ritual: "If one then at once shouts after her: 'In three devils' names, come tomorrow', the Trude must present herself in person the next day. In this way people claim to have discovered many Truden already, who were always old women."
- Corrections against the machine text: "ein Ort" -> "ein Wort"; "Gehülten ausspricht" -> "Gequälten anspricht"; "Fast" -> "Last"; "alte Winter" -> "alte Weiber"; "Föschpapier" -> "Löschpapier".
- Confidence: high (every word read). "kom" has a doubling stroke over the m in the ink, so "komm" is also a correct rendering.
- Caveats: "will man ... entdeckt haben" means "people claim to have discovered"; the physician keeps his distance. The sentence begins on canvas 130, which I downloaded and read: "Diese Druden üben ihre Gewalt auch über andere Menschen, indem sie die Menschen wie mit einer bleiernen Last im Bette überfallen und drücken. Solche von ihnen geplagte Menschen behaupten, daß sie dieselben mit einem Geräusch, wie Löschpapier, Rauschgold heranschleichen und mit zentnerschwerer Last auf sie hinfallen hören." (The sufferers say they hear the Druden creep up with a rustling like blotting paper or tinsel, and fall on them with a hundredweight load.) I left out canvas 134 as instructed.

## Summary

Claims that hold: Eschenbach (Trud-pressing equals nightmare pressure, blamed on witches); Burgau canvas 167 (belief strong, also among those who count themselves educated); Wegscheid canvas 96 (swollen breasts of a child, Trud has suckled, remedy of mirror and nine kinds of dung in the swaddling cloth) and canvas 97 (Trud cross cut into the bedstead); Kötzting (Druden feet on doors, windows, bedsteads, with a small drawn sign); Dingolfing (Trud inborn, witch acquired, both firmly believed, help sought in monasteries); Parsberg canvas 129 (husband's clothing protects the woman in childbed) and canvas 131 (the call that summons the Trud; always old women).

Claims that fail or need a change:
- Burgau canvas 168: "horses found sweating" is not in the ink. The ink says foam at the mouth, tail and mane tousled and tangled, horse faint and exhausted. The machine text misled ("Schweif" is tail, not sweat).
- Lauingen canvas 271: the ink does not say "the Trud sits on the pillow" as a plain statement. A "Hexe" chokes the sleeper; the sleeper throws the pillow on the floor, and the witch stays on it. "Drude oder Hexe" appears only in the seizing clause. She turns into a straw (Strohhalm).
- Wegscheid canvas 96: the physician gives no medical reading. The link to hormonal breast swelling of newborns is the post's interpretation.
- Kötzting: the drawn sign is too small to call a clear pentagram, and the physician's "Celtic Druids" origin is his speculation.

Strongest single hook: Parsberg, canvases 130-131. A district physician in about 1860 records, as a report of sufferers, the three classic features of sleep paralysis: a rustling approach "wie Löschpapier", a hundredweight load on the body, and "unmöglich ... ein Wort zu sprechen", ended the instant another person speaks to the sleeper. Then comes the social sting: the Truden so "discovered" were "immer alte Weiber".
